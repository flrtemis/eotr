What is eotr?

An otter that reminds you to drive.

eotr stands for Eye’s On The Road. We say it like “otter” because the icon is an otter — floating on its back, holding a phone.

Texting while driving is one of the most preventable causes of crashes. eotr doesn’t judge you. It just adds the right friction at the right moment: when the phone detects movement, it shields the screen. When you’re safely parked — flat and still — it relaxes.

The problem

Why this exists

A glance at a phone at 55 mph = blind for ~100 meters. Most people don’t mean to drive distracted — they just don’t have a moment of friction that says “put it down.”

  • Your phone already has what it needs: accelerometer, gyroscope, GPS
  • eotr uses them to tell moving vs. parked
  • It works whether you’re driver or passenger — but never traps passengers
eotr is not punishment. It’s a pause button that protects you and everyone on the road in New Castle County — and everywhere else.
How the rule works

The 10,000 ms rule

eotr only relaxes when all four agree you’re safely parked:

📳 Accel~9.81 m/s²
low shake
🌀 Gyro< 0.12 rad/s
not turning
📡 GPS< 0.5 m/s
~1.1 mph
📐 Leveltilt < 15°
flat surface

They must stay true continuously for 10 seconds. Lift the phone, turn, or start moving → timer resets to 0 and the shield comes back. Why “level”? So a phone in a pocket doesn’t count — you must intentionally place it down.

What you see: Red otter block screen — “Eyes on the road.” Countdown “7.2s still needed.” Emergency SOS always works. Passenger button exists so passengers aren’t stranded, but still requires 10s flat & still.
What eotr does — by platform

Android — Shield any app

Shows a full-screen block over any app while driving is detected. Persistent notification, restarts after reboot. For truly unbreakable, IT can set it as Device Owner for fleet/teen devices.

  • Foreground + overlay + accessibility keep-alive
  • Auto-restarts after reboot
  • Emergency dialer always allowed

iPhone — Apple-approved shield

iPhones don’t allow overlays. eotr uses Apple’s Screen Time FamilyControls to shield the apps you choose (Messages, TikTok, etc.) while driving. You pick which apps.

  • CoreMotion + GPS inside eotr
  • Shields chosen apps while moving
  • SOS never blocked

Without Screen Time approval, eotr still warns inside its own app — but can’t shield other apps. That’s Apple’s limit.

Best-effort vs. unbreakable: On regular phones, eotr creates strong friction — but a determined user can still force-stop it or revoke permission. That’s on purpose: phones must stay honest, not become ransomware. For schools/fleets, MDM/Device Owner makes it truly unbreakable.
Privacy

Your data never leaves your phone.

GPS speed is used in RAM to check “moving vs. still” and then thrown away. Accelerometer/gyro are checked live. No servers, no ads, no tracking, no sale. You can verify: the apps have no network permission for that data.

  • No account required
  • No location history stored
  • Uninstall = everything gone
Is eotr for you?

Great for

  • New drivers + parents of teens
  • Anyone who wants help breaking the glance habit
  • Fleets that want a fleet-wide policy (MDM)
  • Delaware commuters on I-95, Rt 1, Rt 13

Not for

  • Navigation while driving — use a dash mount + voice directions. eotr allows dash mounts in v1.1 option.
  • Emergency calls — those work always, by design.
Safety first

Test parked. Never while driving.

Use the “Test Block Screen” button while parked to see how it feels. Never test while moving. If you’re a passenger, use the Passenger button — but you’ll still need 10 seconds flat & still.

  • Emergency SOS / 911 bypasses eotr — always. Hold side button or tap Emergency on block screen.
  • Passenger mode exists so passengers aren’t trapped, with a warning.
  • Navigation: Start it before you drive, keep phone flat. eotr won’t interrupt voice directions.

eotr adds friction to save lives, but it can’t override human choice. Keep your eyes on the road.
